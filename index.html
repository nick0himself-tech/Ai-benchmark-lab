    How it works

    Everything the site measures, in plain words.

    Scores

    Every answer gets 0 to 3 points: 3 fully correct, 2 mostly, 1 partly, 0 wrong or missing. Harder questions count more (difficulty 1 to 3). A percentage is the points earned divided by the points possible, so 100% means every answer in that group was fully correct.

    Example: two questions of difficulty 1 and 3, scored 3 and 2 → (3×1 + 2×3) ÷ (3×1 + 3×3) = 9 ÷ 12 = 75%.

    How answers are graded

    • Exact and numeric answers are checked automatically against a verified answer key.
    • Format rules (word counts, JSON, banned letters) are checked by code.
    • Code is run against hidden tests in your browser.
    • Open-ended answers are scored against a checklist by you or by a judge model.

    Arena

    Open challenges with no ceiling. Optimisation and golf use an objective number (shorter tour, fewer bins, fewer characters). Builds must pass hidden tests. Bots play every other bot. Visual builds get an Elo-style rating from blind head-to-head votes (start 1000; beating a stronger rival gains more). Professional tasks are judged out of 100 against a checklist, with hidden fact checks. Places earn 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 1 points.

    Fit % (Find a model)

    Your description is turned into needs (coding, accurate facts, writing and so on), each with a weight. Fit is the model's score on the tests behind each need, weighted by how much that need matters. Models with less test coverage for your needs are ranked a little lower.

    Efficiency

    • Words per answer: how much reading a model makes you do. Shorter is better when the score is similar.
    • Cost per 100 answers and seconds per answer: measured on API runs.
    • Leaner option: a model within 10 points of the best that is cheaper or more concise.

    Run settings

    Every run records access (free or paid), interface (chat app or API), thinking and tools. Compare runs only when these match: a free chat app and a paid API with deep thinking are different products.

    Who judges the open-ended answers

    • About 80% of tests are checked automatically: exact answers, numbers and running code. No AI decides those.
    • Open-ended answers go to a panel of three judges from different companies. A model never judges itself or its own family.
    • Judges never see which model wrote the answer. They tick a fixed checklist and are told to judge substance, not typos or formatting.
    • A judge must first agree with at least 85% of answers graded in advance. Weak models can still be tested, but they can't judge.
    • The majority (or middle score) counts, and answers where judges disagree strongly go on a review list.
    Judge statistics appear here once the panel has graded something.

    Limits to keep in mind

    • A few hundred questions give a good signal, but differences of a few points between two models are usually noise.
    • Pasting from chat apps can damage formatting; use "Re-check all" after checker updates.
    • A judge model may favour answers that resemble its own. Use a judge that is not being tested.
    • Public questions can end up in future training data. Fresh challenges in the Arena are the best guard against that.
